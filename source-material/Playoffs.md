To accommodate the expansion from 32 to 48 teams, the 2026 FIFA World Cup will feature 12 groups of four teams, rather than the traditional eight groups.  

This structural shift requires a completely new pathway to the knockout stages. Here is exactly how the new format works and the other major differences you can expect.  
The Third-Place "Playoff"

First, a quick clarification: the third-placed teams do not play a physical playoff match against one another. Instead, at the end of the group stage, all 12 third-placed teams are placed onto a combined virtual leaderboard.

Because the top two teams in each of the 12 groups automatically advance (24 teams), the tournament needs exactly 8 more teams to round out the new 32-team knockout bracket. The top 8 teams on this combined third-place leaderboard advance, while the bottom four are eliminated.  

FIFA uses a strict tiebreaker hierarchy to rank these teams:

    Points: (A win is 3 points, a draw is 1).  

    Goal Difference: The standard first tiebreaker.

    Goals Scored: If goal difference is tied, the team that scored more total goals advances.

    Discipline Points: If still tied, the team with fewer yellow and red cards wins out.  

    FIFA World Ranking: If teams are perfectly identical across all previous metrics, their current FIFA World Ranking will break the tie (this is a new rule for 2026, replacing the old method of randomly drawing lots).
